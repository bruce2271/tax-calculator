# ACME Manufacturing Inc. — a differential test case

A fictional calendar-2024 C corporation, built so the same figures can be keyed into
commercial tax software and compared line by line against this model. Differential
testing against an independent implementation is the only validation here that is not
circular: the Winnebago walk-through derives its inputs from the answer, and the SEC
coverage study measures vocabulary rather than arithmetic.

Load `examples/acme-2024.json` from the sidebar under **Save / Load** to reproduce the
model's column below.

## Why these particular facts

Each choice removes a source of disagreement that would say nothing about the tax logic:

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| No foreign operations, no CFC, not publicly traded | Nothing another package will ask about that this one does not model |
| Dividends from a corporation **under 20% owned** | The DRD rate is the 50% default, so no ownership figure to mis-key |
| Asset placed in service in **2022**, not 2024 | 2024 depreciation is pure MACRS table — no first-year bonus election to disagree about |
| Average gross receipts $8M, well under the §448(c) threshold | §163(j) does not apply, so all interest stays deductible |
| §263A percentages left at zero | Absorption is judgment-dependent and would inject noise |
| Round numbers throughout | An arithmetic slip is visible rather than plausible |

## The arithmetic worth checking by hand

- **Line 15 is 40,000, not 70,000.** A book reserve is not deductible; §166 allows the
  specific charge-off. The 30,000 difference is temporary.
- **Line 8 is zero, not (50,000).** §1211(a) lets capital losses offset only capital
  gains. Short-term 30,000 plus long-term (80,000) plus the 20,000 §1231 gain from Form
  4797 nets to (30,000), which carries forward rather than reducing ordinary income.
- **Form 4797 splits 80,000 of gain.** Adjusted basis is 40,000, so the gain is 80,000.
  §1245 recaptures the depreciation actually taken — 60,000, ordinary. The remaining
  20,000 is the excess of price over original cost, and stays §1231.
- **Line 19 is 86,510.** The §170(b)(2) limit is 10% of taxable income before the
  charitable deduction, the special deductions, and any carryback: 865,100.
- **Line 20 is 174,900.** 7-year MACRS, third recovery year, 17.49% of 1,000,000.
- **Line 18 is the full 300,000.** Average gross receipts are under the §448(c)
  threshold, so §163(j) does not apply at all.
- **Line 29a is 300,000, not 542,872.** The 80% ceiling is not binding — the whole
  carryforward is smaller than the limit.

## Two bugs this test case found before it was ever run elsewhere

Building the fact pattern and hand-checking the model against it surfaced both:

1. **§170(b)(2) base omitted bad debts.** The limit came out 90,510 instead of 86,510,
   overstating the charitable deduction by 4,000 and understating tax by 840.
2. **Book income used the §1211-floored capital figure.** Schedule D line 18 is already
   floored at zero, so book income excluded the disallowed 30,000 loss and Schedule M-1
   line 3 then added it back a second time. The return integrity check caught this — M-1
   landed on 808,590 against a line 28 of 778,590.

Both are pinned in `tests/test_form_1120.py`.

## Where disagreement would be legitimate

Not every difference is a defect. Before treating one as a bug:

- **Depreciation conventions.** This model assumes the half-year convention. A package
  applying the mid-quarter convention — required when more than 40% of the year's
  property is placed in service in the fourth quarter — will differ, correctly.
- **State tax.** This models federal only, plus an optional Texas franchise calculation.
- **The §170 base and NOL ordering.** §170(b)(2)(C) excludes NOL *carrybacks* from the
  base but not carryforwards, which makes the charitable limit and the NOL deduction
  mutually dependent. This model computes the charitable limit before the NOL, which is
  the common simplification rather than the strict reading.
- **Rounding.** Whole dollars against cents.
